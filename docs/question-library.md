# The question library

Fifty-four archetypes across four formats, plus a fifth format that mixes three of them. An **archetype** is one question type — a parameterised template
that produces an unlimited number of distinct items, never the same numbers twice. This is
the catalogue: what each one tests, and a real example generated by the code.

Every example below came out of the generator, not a person. Distractors are labelled with
the wrong method that produces them, and the app shows those labels on the feedback screen —
so a wrong answer tells you *which* shortcut failed, not just that one did.

For how items are built and validated, see [design-rules.md](design-rules.md). For the
controls, see [using-whetstone.md](using-whetstone.md).

## Problem Solving  ·  36

Prose word problems. 18 items in 25 minutes, 83 seconds each.

### Money  ·  9

#### a01 · Budget allocation with a blanket discount

Applying a blanket discount to every line before dividing, then flooring — you cannot buy a fraction of an item.

`money` · warm-up, standard · 83s target · answers as countWithUnit

> Priya handles the lab consumables order and her budget is £365.26. Pipette racks are £31.32
> each, reagent bottles £50.96, glass slides £1.33. All lab stock is 25% off.
>
> **If Priya buys 3 pipette racks and 3 reagent bottles, how many glass slides can she buy?**

**180 slides** — the trap is `135 slides`: discounted the fixed purchases but not the slides:
floor(£180.13 ÷ £1.33)

#### a05 · Payback period

Two unit conversions chained — throughput to revenue per day, then capital cost divided by it. The batch window is the figure people read as an hour.

`money` · standard · 83s target · answers as number · has an estimation route

> Bythorn Components produces 1,195 bushings every 50 minutes and runs for 9 hours a day.
> Bushings are sold by the carton of 27, at £10.58 a carton. A second lathe would cost
> £83,444.
>
> **How many days of output would it take to pay for a second lathe?**

**16.5** — the trap is `0.61`: priced individual bushings at the carton price

#### a13 · Price cut against volume lift

Computing both revenues in full when a price cut meets a volume lift. The sign of the change is the question, and intuition gets it wrong.

`money` · standard · 83s target · answers as signedDirection · has an estimation route

> Quintrell Nursery sells 200 trays a week at £10.46 each. The price is cut by 20%, and the
> number sold rises by 12% as a result.
>
> **What happens to the weekly revenue from this line?**

**Decrease by £217.57** — the trap is `Decrease by £418.40`: applied the discount to the old
revenue and ignored the extra volume

#### a15 · Markup minus fixed overhead

Margin per unit multiplied by unit count, then fixed overhead subtracted. Stopping at the gross margin is the common miss.

`money` · standard, hard · 83s target · answers as currency · has an estimation route

> Follet Diagnostics builds analysers. Each analyser costs £38,600 to build and is sold at a
> markup of 20% on that cost. Fixed overheads for the quarter are £50,000.
>
> **If Follet Diagnostics builds and sells 13 analysers in the quarter, what is its profit for the quarter?**

**£50,360** — the trap is `£7,720`: margin on one unit only, never multiplied by the unit
count

#### d01 · Currency conversion

Choosing whether to multiply or divide by a rate quoted in one explicit direction. The direction of the transaction decides it.

`money` · warm-up, standard · 83s target · answers as currency · has an estimation route

> Tomas is ordering 3 bronze fittings from a foundry in Adelaide. They are listed at £111.00
> each in sterling and the supplier will be paid in Australian dollars. Her bank's board
> quotes £1 buys 1.76 Australian dollars.
>
> **What will the order cost in Australian dollars?**

**A$586.08** — the trap is `A$189.20`: divided by 1.76 where the quote runs the other way, so
this converts Australian dollars into pounds

#### d02 · Currency conversion with a spread

Two rates printed, one applies. A bureau buys low and sells high, so which side you land on depends on which way you are going. A commission sits on top.

`money` · standard, hard · 83s target · answers as currency

> Nuala is changing £1,155.72 into Canadian dollars before a trip. The Gatwick bureau sells
> Canadian dollars at 1.71 to the pound and buys them back at 1.91, and charges 3% commission
> either way.
>
> **How many Canadian dollars will she receive?**

**C$1,916.99** — the trap is `C$1,976.28`: converted the whole amount and never took the 3%
commission off

#### d03 · Break-even units

Dividing fixed costs by contribution margin, then taking the ceiling — you cannot break even on a fraction of a unit. Every distractor is a different denominator.

`money` · warm-up, standard · 83s target · answers as countWithUnit

> Tarnbrook Audio sells each speaker cabinet for £70.17. Materials and labour come to £29.02 a
> speaker cabinet, and the line carries fixed costs of £30,886 a year.
>
> **How many speaker cabinets must it sell in the year to cover its fixed costs?**

**751 speaker cabinets** — the trap is `441 speaker cabinets`: divided by the £70.17 price and
ignored the £29.02 it costs to build one

#### d04 · Margin against markup

Markup is profit over cost; margin is profit over revenue. Given one, find the other. The stem names which is which and which is asked.

`money` · standard, hard · 83s target · answers as percentage

> Ashby Components buys a unit for £45.15. It works to a margin of 42%, where margin is profit
> as a percentage of the selling price.
>
> **What is its markup, that is, profit as a percentage of cost?**

**72.4%** — the trap is `29.6%`: used the other formula on the given figure, dividing by the
wrong base

#### d05 · Tax inclusive against exclusive

Stripping tax by dividing by (1 + rate). Multiplying the gross down by (1 − rate) is close but wrong, by a factor of 4% at a 20% rate.

`money` · warm-up, standard · 83s target · answers as currency

> Bertrand has an invoice from Carraway Print for a run of catalogues. The total is £1,322.30,
> and that figure already includes sales tax at 25%.
>
> **What was the price before tax?**

**£1,057.84** — the trap is `£330.58`: gave the tax itself rather than the figure before tax

### Percentages  ·  4

#### a02 · Chained percentage with a red-herring intermediate

Ignoring an intermediate figure that describes something the question never asks about. Compute the end rate from raw output and compare it to the start.

`percentages` · standard · 83s target · answers as percentage

> Wraysbury Glass was producing 150 panes of cut panes an hour. Over the same period the
> proportion of panes scrapped for edge damage fell by 4%. The line now produces 1,368 panes
> in 8 hours.
>
> **By what percentage has the hourly production rate increased?**

**14%** — the trap is `4%`: reported the 4% figure, which is about something else entirely

#### a19 · Percent change of a percentage

Percentage change OF a percentage. The difference in percentage points is not the percentage change, and both are in the option set.

`percentages` · warm-up, standard · 83s target · answers as percentage

> In March, Aldbury Dairies recorded a contamination rate of 2.25%. In September the
> contamination rate was 2.79%.
>
> **By what percentage did Aldbury Dairies' contamination rate rise between the two?**

**24%** — the trap is `19%`: divided the change by the new value: 0.54 ÷ 2.79

#### a20 · Reverse percentage

Reversing a discount by dividing by its complement. Multiplying back up by the same rate does not return the original.

`percentages` · warm-up · 83s target · answers as currency

> In the trade sale, Ashgrove Tools is selling a mitre saw at 35% off the usual price. The
> sale price is £612.04.
>
> **What was the usual price before the discount?**

**£941.60** — the trap is `£214.21`: took 35% off the sale price instead of reversing it

#### d06 · Stacked discounts

Two discounts multiply, they do not add. Adding the rates always overstates the reduction.

`percentages` · warm-up, standard · 83s target · answers as currency

> A microscope at Loxley Instruments is listed at £414.40. The education discount takes 25%
> off, and a bulk order then takes a further 30% off what is left.
>
> **What is the final price?**

**£217.56** — the trap is `£186.48`: added the rates to 55% and took that off in one go, which
is too much

### Rates  ·  5

#### a03 · "X percent faster" on a composite task

Building a composite time first, then scaling it. Faster means less time, and the unit lives in the stem rather than the options.

`rates` · warm-up, standard · 83s target · answers as number

> A bindery operator takes 35 seconds to prepare a hardback cover and 11 seconds to prepare an
> endpaper. After training, the same worker completes the whole job 22% faster.
>
> **How many seconds will it take to prepare 4 hardback covers and 1 endpapers after the training?**

**117.78** — the trap is `33.22`: reported the time saved rather than the new time

#### a09 · Combined rates, third worker by subtraction

Subtracting a pair rate from a trio rate to isolate one worker, then applying an awkward time. The speed ratio in the stem is a red herring.

`rates` · hard · 83s target · answers as countWithUnit

> At a bindery, Aurelio works 3 times as fast as Kemi. Working together the two of them lay
> 1,440 signatures in 4 hours. With Vance joining them, the three lay 2,625 signatures in 5
> hours.
>
> **How many signatures would Vance lay alone in 5 hours and 20 minutes?**

**880 signatures** — the trap is `825 signatures`: dropped the 20 minutes and used 5 hours

#### d14 · Average speed over two legs, with a unit change

Total distance over total moving time, then a unit conversion. Averaging the two leg speeds is wrong, and rest time is not moving time.

`rates` · warm-up, standard · 83s target · answers as number

> The Kelsmoor swimmers swam the first 400 metres in 380 seconds, rested for 21 seconds, then
> swam the second 400 metres in 220 seconds.
>
> **What was their average speed while moving, in kilometres per hour?**

**4.8**. The trap is `1.3`: left the answer in metres per second, so the conversion to km/h was never applied.

**14.4** — the trap is `4`: left the answer in metres per second, so the conversion to km/h
was never applied

#### d15 · Inverse proportion

More workers means less time. The answer scales inversely with the count, not directly.

`rates` · standard · 83s target · answers as number · has an estimation route

> At Thurlow Groundworks, 8 diggers take 10 days to clear the site. All the diggers work at
> the same rate.
>
> **How long would 10 diggers take to clear the site?**

**8** — the trap is `2`: gave how much the time changes by rather than what the new time is

#### d16 · Dividing in a ratio

Dividing a total by the SUM of the ratio parts, then multiplying by the asked part. Dividing by the number of parts is the trap.

`rates` · warm-up, standard · 83s target · answers as currency · has an estimation route

> £17,955 from the money raised at the Marlbrook appeal is to be divided between the hospice,
> the school and the boat club in the ratio 4:5:10.
>
> **How much does the school receive?**

**£4,725** — the trap is `£3,780`: gave the hospice's share instead

### Comparison  ·  4

#### a06 · Per-unit cost across bulk tiers

Dividing every tier to find the true unit price. The largest pack is deliberately not the cheapest.

`comparison` · warm-up · 83s target · answers as label

> Stourfield Vets sells syringes in five carton sizes: 50 for £11.55, 80 for £16.88, 140 for
> £31.78, 190 for £43.13, 270 for £58.86.
>
> **Which carton size gives the lowest price per syringe?**

**80 for £16.88** — the trap is `50 for £11.55`: took the smallest tier, which is in fact the
dearest at 0.2310 each

#### a12 · Total cost of ownership

Summing every cost line including both labour rates. The trainee wage bill is the line most often dropped, and it is always the largest.

`comparison` · standard · 83s target · answers as label

> Rowan Structural is choosing a CAD suite and has four quotations. The setup fee is charged
> once. Training is then delivered on site: the number of trainers shown attends for the
> number of training hours shown, and 11 design technicians attend as trainees for those same
> hours. Trainers are billed at £27 an hour. Design technicians are paid £14 an hour.  *[table
> follows]*
>
> **Counting the licence fee, the setup fee and all of the training time, which package would cost the least in total?**

**Package C** — the trap is `Package E`: costed the design technicians but left the trainers
out of the wage bill

#### a14 · Throughput times sell-through times price

Units producible per period, then two multipliers. The slowest model to manufacture is the one that wins.

`comparison` · hard · 83s target · answers as label

> Bridgeforth Ceramics runs one kiln line for 10 hours a day, 6 days a week. Each model takes
> the number of minutes shown to make. The proportion shown is the share of everything
> produced that sells. Whatever does not sell brings in nothing.  *[table follows]*
>
> **If the kiln line produced nothing but one model for a whole week, which model would bring in the most money?**

**Model N** — the trap is `Model J`: took the highest number of planters actually sold, 335,
without pricing them

#### a16 · Multi-seller maximum minus minimum

Computing all three seller totals in full. Neither category leader wins, so partial comparison fails.

`comparison` · standard · 83s target · answers as currency · has an estimation route

> Three traders at a farm shop sell the same two lines. Cheese wheels go for £46.12 each and
> cured hams for £37.31. Last month Emeka sold 20 cheese wheels and 18 cured hams, Fenella
> sold 12 and 27, and Janusz sold 18 and 22.
>
> **What is the difference between the highest and the lowest total takings?**

**£90.17** — the trap is `£33.17`: gap between the bottom two sellers

### Averages  ·  5

#### a04 · Fractional deficit plus headcount growth

Two different bases in one item: current stock comes from the current headcount, the requirement from the grown one. Buy the gap.

`averages` · standard · 83s target · answers as countWithUnit · has an estimation route

> Kirkwall Ferries employs 150 deck hands, and every one of them needs deck radio. At the
> moment a sixth of the deck hands do not have one. Headcount is due to rise by 8%.
>
> **How many more deck radios must be bought so that every deck hand has one after the rise?**

**37 deck radios** — the trap is `12 deck radios`: covered only the new deck hands, 162 − 150

#### a21 · Weighted rate across unequal groups

Pooling two groups of unequal size before taking a rate. Averaging the two rates ignores the weights and is wrong.

`averages` · standard · 83s target · answers as percentage

> Norbury entered 25 fixtures last season and won 80% of them. Castlemere entered 20 fixtures
> and won 35%.
>
> **Taken together, what percentage of the 45 fixtures were won?**

**60%** — the trap is `35%`: reported the second group's rate unchanged

#### d08 · Missing value given a mean

Recovering a total from a mean, then stripping the known values. Remembering to multiply by the count is the whole item.

`averages` · warm-up, standard · 83s target · answers as number

> Ledsham Press handled a mean of 489 print runs a month across 6 months. The figures for 5 of
> them were Monday 378, Tuesday 571, Wednesday 295, Thursday 442, Friday 675.
>
> **How many print runs were handled on Saturday?**

**573** — the trap is `394`: divided the values that are given by the count instead of working
back from the total

#### d09 · Effect on a mean of adding an item

Recomputing a mean after one item joins: old total plus new value, over one more than the old count. Dividing by the old count is the off-by-one.

`averages` · warm-up, standard · 83s target · answers as number

> Across its 10 advisers, the Calderbank branch averaged 552 accounts each last season. A new
> adviser then joined, with 155 accounts.
>
> **What is the mean across all 11 of them?**

**515.9** — the trap is `353.5`: averaged the old mean with the new figure as though the 10
existing values counted once between them

#### d10 · Median against mean

Sorting before taking a middle. The mean, the mode and the midrange are all in the option set, and the unsorted middle is too.

`averages` · warm-up, standard · 83s target · answers as number

> Haldon Library recorded the number of loans handled on each of six days: Monday 641, Tuesday
> 825, Wednesday 965, Thursday 649, Friday 825, Saturday 333.
>
> **What was the median number of loans handled per day?**

**737** — the trap is `649`: gave the midrange, halfway between the highest and the lowest

### Series  ·  4

#### a11 · Compounding to a cumulative threshold

Compounding month by month until a running total crosses a threshold. The threshold applies to the cumulative figure, not the monthly one.

`series` · hard · 83s target · answers as month

> Bellhaven Studios made £9,500 profit in May, and expects profit to grow by 4% every month
> after that.
>
> **In which month will total profit since May first reach £73,231?**

**November** — the trap is `October`: stopped at the last month still below the target,
£63,013

#### a22 · Compound depreciation

Repeated multiplication by (1 − r). Multiplying r by the number of years is simple depreciation and always overstates the loss.

`series` · standard · 83s target · answers as currency

> Castleton Farms bought a combine header for £26,545. The header loses 12% of its value every
> year, measured against what it was worth at the start of that year.
>
> **What will the header be worth after 3 years?**

**£18,090** — the trap is `£16,989`: simple depreciation, £26,545 × (1 − 12% × 3)

#### d07 · Compound interest on growth

Repeated multiplication by (1 + r). The simple-interest figure is in the option set and always understates.

`series` · warm-up, standard · 83s target · answers as currency

> £38,705.56 is placed in a reserve account with the Ledbury Fund. It grows at 9% a year, and
> the interest is added to the account each year.
>
> **What will the account be worth after 6 years?**

**£64,913.10** — the trap is `£38,705.56`: reported what was paid in, so the growth was never
applied at all

#### d17 · Compound annual growth rate

The nth root of a growth ratio, less one. Dividing total growth by the number of years ignores compounding and always overstates.

`series` · hard · 83s target · answers as percentage

> Pellow Analytics reported subscriptions of 4,141 at the start of the period and 13,278 7
> years later.
>
> **What was the compound annual growth rate over the period?**

**18.1%** — the trap is `9.8%`: took the growth as a share of the final figure rather than the
starting one, then divided by the years

### Fractions  ·  1

#### a08 · Fraction sequence against the original baseline

Tracking a remaining quantity while the target is a multiple of the ORIGINAL, not of the remainder. The baseline is the trap.

`fractions` · standard, hard · 83s target · answers as fraction · has an estimation route

> At a brewery, a tank holds 5/8 of a barrel of wort. 1/3 of the wort in it is drawn off, and a
> further 1/6 of a barrel is lost to a spill. The tank must end up holding 60% more wort than it
> held to begin with.
>
> **How much wort, as a fraction of a barrel, must be added?**

**3/4** — the trap is `1/4`: reported what is left, 1/4, not the amount to add

### Algebra  ·  2

#### a07 · Simultaneous equations in prose

Cancelling common terms across an equality before substituting. Setting up three full equations runs out the clock.

`algebra` · hard · 83s target · answers as number

> At a chandlery, 5 coils of rope, 5 drums of resin and 1 crate of shackles together balance
> exactly against 1 coil of rope, 1 drum of resin and 8 crates of shackles. Separately, 2
> drums of resin and 1 crate of shackles weigh 42 kilograms in total, and one drum of resin
> weighs 13 kilograms.
>
> **How many kilograms does one coil of rope weigh?**

**15** — the trap is `16`: reported the weight of a crate of shackles, which the question
gives directly

#### a18 · Solving backwards for unknown units

Working backwards: strip every known contribution from a stated total, then divide the residue by a per-unit figure. Two independent counts share one table.

`algebra` · hard · 83s target · answers as countWithUnit

> Craigmoor Plant books a profit on every one of its machines sold, shown as a percentage of
> that model's sale price. It books a further bonus on every extended warranty sold, also as a
> percentage of that model's sale price. Warranties are sold separately from the machines
> themselves, so the two counts are unrelated.  *[table follows]*
>
> **Profit for the year, warranty bonuses included, came to £319,000 in total. How many Drovers were sold?**

**10 Drovers** — the trap is `8 Drovers`: used the Boxer profit rate of 25% on the Drovers
instead of 20%

### Charts  ·  2

#### a10 · Single bar chart with per-bar loss rates

Applying a per-bar loss rate before comparing. The tallest bar is not the answer and neither is the lowest loss rate.

`charts` · warm-up, standard · 83s target · answers as label

> Craymoor Bank recorded how many applications came from each source this cycle, and what
> percentage of each was rejected at the screening stage before the assessment centre.  *[chart follows]*
>
> **Which source produced the most applications that reached the assessment centre?**

**Spring week** — the trap is `Graduate portal`: fourth on survivors, so no shortcut and no
near miss lands on it

#### a17 · Grouped bar chart to group totals to a margin

Summing bars into group totals, differencing two groups, then applying a margin to the difference. Differencing first is the shortcut worth learning.

`charts` · standard, hard · 83s target · answers as currency · has an estimation route

> Bellamy Textiles reports quarterly sales for four years. Any increase in sales carries a 12%
> shipping surcharge and a 25% wholesaler margin, both deducted from the increase.  *[chart
> follows]*
>
> **What did Bellamy Textiles retain from the rise in total sales between 2020 and 2023?**

**$5.36m** — the trap is `$3.47m`: differenced 2020 against 2021 rather than 2023

## Data Interpretation  ·  11

Tables and charts, often with one stimulus serving several questions in a row. 20 items in 15 minutes, 45 seconds each.

### Comparison  ·  4

#### b02 · Counterfactual on a single cell

Perturbing one cell, then evaluating a ratio on the modified data. Reading the ratio straight off the table is the trap.

`comparison` · warm-up, standard · 45s target · answers as ratio · has an estimation route

> The table shows how many referrals of each type are recorded at each of the 4 wards. Every
> referral is counted in exactly one category.  *[table follows]*
>
> **If the number of Renal at Osgood Ward were reduced by 25%, what would the ratio of Renal to Oncology and Neurology combined at Osgood Ward then be?**

**7:6** — the trap is `6:7`: gave the ratio the other way round

#### b03 · Counterfactual that changes the denominator

Perturbing a cell that also sits inside the total it is measured against, so the denominator moves with it. The item is whether you propagate the change.

`comparison` · standard · 45s target · answers as percentage

> The table shows how many referrals of each type are recorded at each of the 4 wards. Every
> referral is counted in exactly one category.  *[table follows]*
>
> **If the number of Renal at Petrie Ward were reduced by 60%, what percentage of the referrals at Petrie Ward would then be Oncology?**

**32.19%** — the trap is `5.86%`: took the perturbed Renal figure as the numerator instead of
Oncology

#### b04 · Counterfactual under a conservation constraint

One cell rises and another must fall to hold a total constant. Find the required percentage fall — measured against the second cell, not the total.

`comparison` · hard · 45s target · answers as percentage · has an estimation route

> The table shows how many referrals of each type are recorded at each of the 4 wards. Every
> referral is counted in exactly one category.  *[table follows]*
>
> **If Vellacott Ward increases its Cardiology by 120%, by what percentage would it have to reduce its Neurology to keep its total number of referrals the same?**

**70%** — the trap is `19.5%`: took the increase of 109.2 as a percentage of the whole
Vellacott Ward total of 560

#### b06 · Argmax or argmin over a derived series

Finding an argmax or argmin over a series you have to derive first. The largest raw value in the table is not the answer.

`comparison` · standard · 45s target · answers as label

> Halloway Paints sells paint in 5 sizes. The table shows how many of each size were sold last
> year, together with the price, the cost and the profit on a single unit.  *[table follows]*
>
> **Which size made the most total profit last year?**

**2.5 litre** — the trap is `250ml`: worked out total profit correctly and then took the
smallest

### Charts  ·  2

#### c01 · Pie chart, share shown and absolute asked

Converting a pie share into an absolute count. The total appears only in the caption, so the item cannot be answered without reading it.

`charts` · warm-up, standard · 45s target · answers as number · has an estimation route

> Ashgrove Clinic categorised every one of its referrals in the last quarter.  *[chart
> follows]*
>
> **How many referrals were recorded as dermatology?**

**9,688** — the trap is `56`: reported the 56% share itself where the question asks for a
count of referrals

#### c02 · Two pies, share falls while absolute rises

Two pies with different totals. A share can fall while the absolute value rises, which is the strongest trap the format offers.

`charts` · hard · 45s target · answers as label

> Thornlea College recorded its applications by category in 2020 and again in 2025. The total
> for each year is given beside its chart.  *[chart follows]*
>
> **Which category grew the most in absolute terms between 2020 and 2025?**

**Design** — the trap is `Engineering`: took the largest FALL in share, 32% to 13%, reading
the comparison the wrong way round

### Normalising  ·  5

#### b01 · Stimulus-level unit scaling

Scaling every figure from a per-100g basis to actual pack sizes before summing. One multiplication that a candidate in a hurry skips.

`normalising` · warm-up, standard · 45s target · answers as number

> Every figure in the table is the amount contained in 100 g of that variety. The bottom row
> gives the weight of one portion as sold.  *[table follows]*
>
> **How much salt, in grams, is contained in 3 portions of Bran Flakes?**

**1.26** — the trap is `0.42`: scaled one portion correctly but never multiplied by the 3
portions asked for

#### b05 · Shortfall or excess against a reference

Comparing a computed quantity against a stated reference and reporting the gap. The option set mixes numbers with verdict strings, so direction matters.

`normalising` · standard, hard · 45s target · answers as verdict

> Every figure in the table is the amount contained in 100 g of that variety. The bottom row
> gives the weight of one portion as sold.  *[table follows]*
>
> **By how many percent would someone eating 4 portions of Bran Flakes have fallen short of the recommended daily amount of salt?**

**Cannot Say** — the trap is `93%`: measured against the fibre reference of 24 g, which is not
the nutrient asked about

#### b07 · Row share against column share

Two near-identical questions, one asking for a share along the row and one along the column. Both values appear in both option sets.

`normalising` · warm-up, standard · 45s target · answers as percentage

> The table shows how many referrals of each type are recorded at each of the 4 wards. Every
> referral is counted in exactly one category.  *[table follows]*
>
> **What percentage of the referrals at Vellacott Ward are Neurology?**

**27.86%** — the trap is `6.35%`: divided by the grand total of 2457 rather than by one line
of it

#### b08 · Derived series before aggregation

Building a derived series across every column, aggregating it, then taking a share. The most expensive item in the format: five multiplications, a sum and a division.

`normalising` · hard · 45s target · answers as percentage

> Kirkmoor Tyres sells tyres in 5 sizes. The table shows how many of each size were sold last
> year, together with the price, the cost and the profit on a single unit.  *[table follows]*
>
> **What percentage of all takings last year were due to 17 inch tyres?**

**20.08%** — the trap is `13.99%`: gave the 17 inch share of tyres sold and never multiplied
by price

#### d13 · Per capita normalisation

Ranking on a per-head figure rather than a raw total. Per capita ordering differs from total ordering, which is the entire point.

`normalising` · standard · 45s target · answers as label

> The table shows capital spending in 2019 and 2024 for 7 transport authorities, together with
> the resident population each one serves. Population is unchanged between the two years.
> *[table follows]*
>
> **Which transport authority spent the most per resident in 2024?**

**Belmarch** — the trap is `Denhollow`: read the largest population instead of working out the
figure per head

## Reading the catalogue

**Tiers** filter which items an archetype produces, not which archetypes exist: the same
template appears in warm-up with light arithmetic and in hard with awkward numbers.

**Target seconds** is what the pace score is measured against, not a limit. Only Tempo and
Classify enforce a per-item clock.

**Estimation route** marks the thirteen archetypes that can show the approximate path to the
answer alongside the exact one. On a clock the estimate is often the better method, and the
feedback screen puts it above the exact chain for that reason.

**The trap** shown with each example is the single most instructive distractor. Every item
carries four, each from a named wrong procedure.

## Deductive Reasoning  ·  4

Orders, groups and if-then rules. 12 items in 18 minutes, 90 seconds each. Every item is solved by enumerating
every arrangement its clues allow, and every wrong option is what a named misreading of one clue leads to.

### Sequencing  ·  2

#### l01 · Sequencing: who is on which day

Five people across five weekdays, one each. The clues settle one fact, asked either as who is on a given day or as which day a given person is on.

`sequencing` · warm-up, standard, hard · 90s target · answers as label

> Five candidates, Chloe, Dev, Elena, Sami and Vera, are each interviewed once next week. There is exactly one interview each day, Monday to Friday.
>
> 1. Vera is interviewed the day before Chloe.
> 2. Dev is interviewed on an earlier day than Sami.
> 3. Dev is interviewed on either Monday or Friday.
> 4. There is exactly one day between Elena's interview and Vera's interview.
> 5. Vera is interviewed on an earlier day than Sami.
>
> **Who is interviewed on Thursday?**

**Elena**. The trap is `Chloe`: read "exactly one day between" as one day apart.

#### l02 · Sequencing: what must or cannot be true

The clues leave between two and six orders open. Which statement holds in every one of them, or in none? The trap is a statement that fits the one arrangement you happened to find.

`sequencing` · standard, hard · 90s target · answers as label

> Five candidates, Aisha, Dev, Farid, Hugo and Leon, are each interviewed once next week. There is exactly one interview each day, Monday to Friday.
>
> 1. Aisha is interviewed on an earlier day than Dev.
> 2. There are exactly two days between Dev's interview and Leon's interview.
> 3. Hugo is interviewed on an earlier day than Aisha.
> 4. Aisha is interviewed on an earlier day than Farid.
>
> **Which of the following must be true?**

**Aisha is interviewed on Wednesday.**. The trap is `Hugo is interviewed the day before Aisha.`: true in 1 of the 2 orders, so it could be true but need not be.

### Grouping  ·  1

#### l03 · Grouping: who must be together

Six people in two groups of three. Who must share a group with a named person, sometimes after a supposition fixes someone first?

`grouping` · warm-up, standard, hard · 90s target · answers as label

> Six new joiners, Dev, Grace, Hugo, Nikhil, Rosa and Vera, are split into two project teams of three: the North team and the South team.
>
> 1. Vera and Dev are not on the same team.
> 2. Rosa and Dev are not on the same team.
>
> **Who must be on the same team as Rosa?**

**Vera**. The trap is `Dev`: missed the "not" in "Rosa and Dev are not on the same team".

### Conditionals  ·  1

#### l04 · Conditional reasoning: what follows

A chain of if-then rules and one fact. What must follow, including the items where nothing does.

`conditionals` · warm-up, standard, hard · 90s target · answers as label

> Assume all of the following statements are true.
>
> 1. If the client renews the contract, then the new system goes live.
> 2. If the new system goes live, then the budget is approved.
> 3. If the new system goes live, then the report is not published on time.
> 4. The report is published on time.
>
> **Which of the following must also be true?**

**The client does not renew the contract.**. The trap is `The budget is not approved.`: denies the antecedent: the "if" part failing tells you nothing about the "then" part.

## Inductive Reasoning  ·  3

Number, letter and figure series. 15 items in 18 minutes, 72 seconds each. A series is shown only if no other rule
in the library fits the same terms and predicts something else.

### Number series  ·  1

#### i01 · Number series: the next term

Five numbers under a hidden rule; give the sixth. The rule is the only one in the library that fits all five.

`number-series` · warm-up, standard, hard · 72s target · answers as number

> 11,  33,  39,  117,  123,  ?
>
> **Which number comes next in the series?**

**369**. The trap is `129`: assumed the last difference repeats, so the rule was read as adding a constant.

### Letter series  ·  1

#### i02 · Letter series: the next code

Letters under a hidden rule, and from standard tier codes such as C4, F7, I10 with a rule for each part. Give the next term.

`letter-series` · warm-up, standard, hard · 72s target · answers as label

> I4,  Y7,  E10,  V13,  A16,  ?
>
> **Which code comes next in the series?**

**S19**. The trap is `S16`: got the letter right but did not move the number on.

### Figure series  ·  1

#### i03 · Figure series: the next figure

Five figures, each changing in up to four independent ways. Pick the sixth. Every wrong figure gets exactly one change wrong.

`figure-series` · warm-up, standard, hard · 72s target · answers as label

> Each figure follows from the one before it by the same rules.
>
> 1. grey arrow pointing down-right, triangle top right
> 2. black arrow pointing up-right, circle top right
> 3. white arrow pointing up-left, square top right
> 4. grey arrow pointing down-left, triangle top right
> 5. black arrow pointing down-right, circle top right
>
> **Which figure comes next?**

**white arrow pointing up-right, square top right**. The trap is `black arrow pointing up-right, square top right`: kept the arrow's shading as it was in the fifth figure: black instead of white.
